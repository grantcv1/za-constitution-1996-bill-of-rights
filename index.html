The Constitution of South Africa, 1996 in Akoma Ntoso

The Preamble and Chapter 2, the Bill of Rights, of the Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996, as first published in the Government Gazette on December 18, 1996. The document is marked up in Akoma Ntoso, the OASIS standard for legal and legislative documents.

View the Constitution

Why this document

This is the constitution that replaced apartheid. A Constitutional Assembly elected in 1994, in the country’s first democratic elections, wrote it. President Nelson Mandela signed it into law at Sharpeville, the site of the 1960 massacre, on December 10, 1996, and it took effect on February 4, 1997. The Bill of Rights in this extract calls itself a cornerstone of democracy in South Africa, and it protects the rights of all people in the country.

On a personal note, this constitution is important to me because South Africa is the country of my birth.

How it looks

The page sets the text as the Gazette printed it: the same pages, headings and line breaks, including the Table of Non-Derogable Rights. The XML records every line and page break with the standard’s own eol and eop elements. It is reset type, not a facsimile.

How it was made

The XML was generated by Claude for Grant Vergottini. The text comes from the scan of Government Gazette No. 17678 published by the South African Government. Claude corrected the scan’s OCR errors and checked the result against it. This is an extract, and it is the text as enacted, not as later amended. It is not an official version.
